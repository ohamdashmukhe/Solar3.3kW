Method: Daily energy = 3.3 kW × performance ratio × irradiance (kWh/m², from Open-Meteo's tilted-plane radiation for this exact tilt/orientation), with a temperature de-rate applied hour by hour before summing to a daily total. The de-rate uses your actual panel spec — Rayzon RSB550WC, power temperature coefficient -0.321%/°C — applied not to raw air temperature but to an NOCT-modeled cell temperature (NOCT 44.28°C per the datasheet), since panels run well above ambient air temperature in direct sun; a 35°C Pune afternoon with strong irradiance can put the cells near 55-60°C, which raw ambient temperature alone would miss. Irradiance is blended across three independent weather models (ECMWF IFS, NOAA GFS, DWD ICON) rather than a single source, which reduces bias from any one model's error. The range shown per day combines two independent sources of uncertainty: how much the three weather models disagree for that day, and how much your own logged performance ratio has varied historically (its recency-weighted mean ± spread). If a model is unreachable, the dashboard falls back to a single model with a synthetic ±10% band, flagged in the weather-source spec. The performance ratio itself is not a manual setting — it's calculated from your logged actual-vs-predicted readings (bundling inverter, wiring, and soiling losses together, since temperature is now handled separately at the hourly, cell-temperature level), weighted toward your most recent entries, and recalculated every time you save or import a reading. With fewer than 2 logged readings it falls back to a default of 0.75 ± 0.12. Weather data covers roughly 92 days back and 16 days ahead of today; dates outside that window show as pending. Note: the RSB550WC is a bifacial module (transparent backsheet, ~75% rear-side gain factor per datasheet) — this dashboard currently models front-side output only, since rear-side gain depends heavily on your actual roof clearance and surface reflectivity underneath the panels, which isn't captured by weather data alone.
